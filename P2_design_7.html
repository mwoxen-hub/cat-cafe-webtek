Client

Our client is Cathrine Cat. She is the owner of Purr & Pour, a cat cafe dedicated to animal rights and mental health.

Email: cathrine@purrandpour.com

Phone: +47 270 19 367

From our last meeting October 2nd 2026: Cathrine told us that she wants us to make a clean look with soft green colors that represent the planet, and rounded features. She also mentioned that she wants the main goal to be that it is easy for visitors to find information about the cats and the cafe. Furthermore, she also wants it to be easy for people to adopt cats by the website, and to get all the information that they need.

Purpose, goals and audience

Our purpose is to create a website that promotes the cat cafe and its values, while providing information about the cats, menu, and policies.

The goals are to increase awareness of the rescued cats, where they can find a loving home, or just feel comfortable and safe. Furthermore, we aim to educate visitors about the importance of adopting cats and supporting animal welfare.

Audience

  • Individuals: People who love cats and want to spend time with them. Many want to get to know the cats before they meet them at the cafe. The Cats page shows this.
  • Families and friends: Those who want to look at the menu and book a reservation before going to the cafe. The Menu and Booking pages show this.
  • People seeking stress relief and a peaceful atmosphere. We want a website that will provide a calming experience for our users. The website will explain how the environment can help reduce stress. The About page shows this.
  • Organizations and people: Those who are interested in animal welfare and supporting rescue organizations. Here you can find information about our partnerships and how to get involved. The Contact page shows this.
  • Pet adopters: they need to see each cat's age and personality, and know how to apply. The Cats page shows this, with a link to the adoption form.

Our client Cathrine Cat wanted a clean look with soft colors that represent the planet. We therefore chose a calm palette of soft greens on a warm off-white background, with green used as an accent for headings, links and borders. To make the text easy to read, we use dark grey for the body text and a darker green for links, as the original soft green did not have enough contrast against the background.

Navigation bar, header and footer

Navigation bar: The navigation bar will be visible on all pages and will contain our 6 links: Home, About, Cats, Menu, Booking, and Contact. The active page is shown in semibold. On screens under 700px, the navigation bar will be replaced by a hamburger menu.

Header: The header lies at the top of the page and the color of the header is #FFFFFF (white). The header will contain the website's name and logo.

Footer: The footer lies at the bottom of every page. It shows the café's contact information (email and phone number) and links to Instagram and Facebook, so visitors can easily get in touch. At the very bottom, a small line created by footer.js shows the names of the developers.

Minimum requirements

Different JavaScript-functions

We are having 4 different JavaScripts, nav.js which is the navigation bar, footer.js that shows our names in the bottom of every file. A hover.js that enlarges the “adopt me” button. The last JavaScript file we will have is a validation for the booking.html, which checks if you have filled in the two buttons.

Four distinct responsive components

  1. We have the navbar, when the screen-size is under 700px the navbar turns into a burger-menu.
  2. We are also planning on using grid to scale the cat-cards. For example: we have 3 on computer-screens, 2 on ipads and 1 on phones.
  3. The third component is that the pictures will change size to adjust to the screen size, this will also make the usability improve.
  4. The last component we are planning on is that the textboxes will change according to the layout. We can use Flexbox and flex-wrap to make this work.

How we are using CSS for the layout now

We use CSS grid for the header. The .meny class is a grid with three columns (grid-template-columns) and the .nav-grid class uses grid-template-columns: auto auto to lay out the navigation links.
The .info class uses Flexbox, display:flex with gap:2rem, so the author, client and date on the P1 HTML document line up, side by side.
We also use width, padding, margin and border to change the layout of content boxes. The textbox1 and textbox2-classes both use width:90%, max-width: 1500px and padding 20px so they dont get to wide for the window. We have also use border and border-radius to give a softer look. All layout is now written in one stylesheet, style.css.

Accessibility measures

ARIA- Accessible Rich Internet Applications, it's a set of attributes you add to HTML to share extra information to the screen readers that necessarily isn't clear from just the code itself.
In the nav.js we have used aria-label that gives an element a name that the screen reader reads out.
aria-expanded tells us if something is open or closed. button aria-expanded="false/true"
aria-current=“page” marks the link to the page the user is on, a href="booking.html" aria-current="page">Booking here the screen reader will say “Booking, current page.”

Alt text on images – every <img&g has an alt that describes the image, for example: alt=“Toffe,a Scottish fold cat”

Labels- labels are what points to the id of the input. For example in the booking.html, the <label for="email"> Email > will make the screen reader read “Email”

Visible focus for keyboard users- we are using CSS to make an outline when you navigate with Tab through the website.

Contrast between text and background – we have made the font color a color that will pass the WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) requirements.
