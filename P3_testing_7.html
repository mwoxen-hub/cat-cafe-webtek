What did our live testers dislike?

Our live testers were a big help in finding small inconveniences on our site. Two of our three live testers struggled to write their phone number in the correct format the form requires. Also, one of our live testers struggled to locate the adoption button immediately. The adoption form also didn't have an option of no previous pets which confused some of the users, since they didn't know it could be left blank.

What did they like?

Our live testers were almost unanimous in saying the website was clean and well organized, and also scoring 4.5 on a 1-5 scale on navigation. One live tester enjoyed how much information that was displayed on the site.

All areas scored high. Design got the lowest average, and it is also where we got the most criticism.

Positive feedback

  1. Cats: The photos and the personal text about each cat were the most mentioned positives. One tester wrote that the biographies make a visit more exciting because you can recognise the cats.
  2. Clean and easy to use: Many testers described the website as clear, simple and easy to navigate. One wrote "fine design, easy to find everything, pictures of the cats, a very clean website"
  3. Calm look: Several said the design and the colors fit the theme of a cozy cafe.

Design

  1. Around nine comments called the colors a bit boring and wanted more color or stronger contrast.
  2. One tester suggested fewer white boxes for more variation, one text color instead of both black and green, and bold headlines
  3. One tester suggested a large picture or illustration at the top of the front page to make an atmosphere and to show right away that this is a cafe with cats. Another wrote that before they connected the name and the cats, they did not quite understand what the site was.

Navigation

  1. One tester could not easily go back to the home page, for example after looking at the menu
  2. One tester wanted a booking button on the front page
  3. One tester wrote that some pages have a lot on them and wanted to click further in, instead of having everything on one page

Content

  1. One tester wrote that opening hours and location should come before the cats on the front page
  2. One tester was unsure about showing menu items on the front page and wanted it to focus on the cozy atmosphere. Others liked the menu
  3. One tester wanted links to the reviews, the food safety rating and house rules, for example if you could bring dogs or pick up the cats.
  4. One tester wanted a Norwegian version of the site

Usability

  1. One tester had to try three times before the booking accepted a time, and wanted to know from the start when it is possible to book.

Responsiveness

  1. Three testers mentioned the menu on a phone: it looked a bit odd, was almost out at the edge of the screen, or was not centered on the front page.
  2. Some testers wrote that everything fit their screen well.

Accessibility

  1. Contrast: One tester pointed out that the light green descriptions on the menu page have too low contrast. Another found poor contrast on the About page, and others asked for a darker text color or noted small text.
  2. Menu filter: One tester pointed out that the category buttons only show visually which category is selected, so screen readers do not get this information.
  3. Booking form: One tester wrote that the form should use standard field labels (autocomplete) so that the browser can fill in name and email automatically.
  4. Dark mode: One tester wrote that the website didn't support dark mode.

Our response

We read through all the feedback from the form we made, and decided to go with the following changes:

  1. One of our testers wrote that the light green descriptions on the menu page were difficult to read. We changed them to a darker green, which raised the contrast from 3.57:1 to 5.97:1 and now meets the WCAG requirement.
  2. Further, a person had a problem with the booking hours, and had to try three times before it got confirmed. We fixed the problem by showing which hours you can schedule a booking, before you choose the booking time.
  3. We also decided to add a new button to our homepage, which says "Book a table", since one tester wanted to book directly from the front page.
  4. A tester responded that you couldn't autofill your name and email on the booking page. Therefore, we added autocomplete to make it possible to autofill your name and email.
  5. We also added an aria-pressed attribute on the filter buttons on the menu and cats page, since a tester pointed out that screen readers couldn't tell which category is chosen.
  6. Last but not least, two of our three live testers struggled to write their phone number in the right format. We changed the phone number field, so that you are able to write both 12345678 and 123 45 678.

The changes we decided not to go with were first of all changing our colors. Our client Cathrine wanted these specific colors to match the cafe, and the vibe. Everyone is different, and not everyone likes the same colors. Some of our testers pointed out that they had an issue with the menu on their phone. They told us that the menu was a bit crooked, and unfortunately we weren't able to fix that in time. The last thing many testers commented on was that people would like to see a picture of the cafe on the front page. We didn't go with that change, since this is not a real café, and decided to focus on other things rather than portraying a picture of a fake café.

We didn't have an adoption button in the navigation menu, so it wasn't as easy to find as the other links and buttons. It was probably a bit more difficult to find the link to where you can fill out a form and adopt a cat. Later, we could add "Adoption" to the menu. Some live testers were also confused that the adoption form didn't have an option for no previous pets. We could add a "None" option, or a short text saying that you can leave it blank.

Self reflection

We were quite happy with getting such a good response, especially since this is our first time making a website. We were a bit surprised that some had issues with a crooked menu, since we have been trying to make sure that every page works well for phone users.

Next time, before we send out the form, we should write a more descriptive text. Some of our testers didn't go through everything, and then had to go back to the website, and then back to the form and fill in everything again, since they didn't know that they were supposed to go through all of it. They suggested adding a "didn't try that" option to the form.

We were also surprised that most of the comments were about wanting brighter colors. However, that is not the worst feedback, because it is about colors and not big issues with the website itself. Next time, we would maybe go through the testing earlier, and check the contrast and accessibility on the site along the way. The pictures were also quite large and could make the website slow, so we made them around 70% smaller than they originally were.

It was really fun to get people to test a website we have made ourselves, and especially to get their feedback. It made us want to do this more often, for example for other clients who would like a nice website.

Use of AI

All of us used AI assistants while working on the website and this report. We used them as a tutor: to explain concepts, find bugs and validation errors, suggest improvements and correct language. In some cases the AI gave us example code or text, but it also explained what the code does and why, so that we understood it before we used it. We made all the decisions ourselves, wrote most of the code and text ourselves, and learned a lot from working this way.
